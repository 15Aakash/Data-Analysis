# HR Employee Attrition Analysis Dashboard using Excel

## Project Overview

This project analyzes **1,470 employee records using Microsoft Excel** to identify employee attrition patterns and workforce groups with higher turnover risk.

The analysis evaluates attrition across **departments, job roles, age groups, salary bands, gender, and overtime status**, while comparing both the **number of employee departures** and the **actual attrition rate** within each group.

This distinction is important because a group with a high number of departures does not necessarily have the highest attrition risk if that group also has a larger employee population.

The final deliverable is an **interactive Excel dashboard** featuring KPI cards, PivotTables, PivotCharts, slicers, and a dedicated Business Insights section that translates workforce data into actionable HR recommendations.

---

## Business Objective

The objective of this project is to help HR teams:

- Measure overall employee attrition.
- Identify workforce groups with higher attrition risk.
- Compare employee departure volumes with actual attrition rates.
- Understand patterns related to department, role, age, salary, and overtime.
- Support data-driven employee retention planning.

## Key Insights

### Overall Attrition

**237 employees left out of 1,470 employees**, resulting in an overall attrition rate of **16.12%**.

### Department Analysis

Research & Development recorded the **largest number of employee departures with 133 employees leaving**.

However, **Sales had the highest departmental attrition rate at 20.63%**.

This demonstrates why analyzing attrition rate alongside departure count is important.

### Job Role Analysis

Laboratory Technicians recorded the **largest number of departures with 62 employees leaving**.

However, **Sales Representatives had the highest job-role attrition rate at 39.76%**.

This indicates that Sales Representatives represent a particularly high-risk employee group despite not having the largest number of departures.

### Age Group Analysis

Employees aged **25–34 recorded the largest number of employee departures**.

However, employees **under the age of 25 had the highest attrition rate at 39.18%**.

This suggests that younger employees may require additional attention when developing retention strategies.

### Salary Band Analysis

Employees in the **Low Salary** category had the highest attrition rate at **28.61%**.

For comparison:

- Low Salary: **28.61%**
- Medium Salary: **12.03%**
- High Salary: **10.80%**

The results show a clear difference in attrition across compensation groups.

### Overtime Analysis

Employees working overtime experienced an attrition rate of **30.53%**, compared with only **10.44%** among employees who did not work overtime.

This indicates a strong association between overtime and employee attrition and suggests that workload may be an important area for further investigation.

---

## Key Analytical Takeaway

One of the most important findings from this project was that:

> **The group with the highest number of departures is not always the group with the highest attrition rate.**

For example:

- Research & Development had the most departmental departures, but **Sales had the highest departmental attrition rate**.
- Laboratory Technicians had the most job-role departures, but **Sales Representatives had the highest job-role attrition rate**.
- Employees aged 25–34 had the most departures by age group, but **employees under 25 had the highest age-group attrition rate**.

Analyzing both metrics provides a more accurate picture of employee turnover risk.

---

## Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, HR teams could consider the following actions:

- Investigate compensation and retention strategies for **low-salary employees**, who experienced significantly higher attrition.
- Conduct deeper analysis of **Sales Representatives and other high-attrition job roles** to identify potential causes of turnover.
- Develop targeted retention initiatives for **younger employees**, particularly employees under the age of 25.
- Review overtime policies and workload distribution because employees working overtime showed substantially higher attrition.
- Develop department-specific retention strategies, particularly within **Sales**.
- Investigate additional factors such as career progression, job satisfaction, management relationships, and promotion opportunities within high-risk employee groups.

These recommendations represent areas for further investigation and should not be interpreted as confirmed causes of employee turnover.

## Conclusion

The analysis identified meaningful differences in employee attrition across departments, job roles, age groups, salary levels, and overtime status.

While some groups recorded a large number of employee departures, calculating group-level attrition rates revealed that the highest departure volume did not always represent the highest turnover risk.

The findings suggest that HR retention efforts could prioritize **younger employees, low-salary groups, high-risk job roles, employees working overtime, and the Sales department**, while conducting deeper analysis to understand the underlying factors associated with employee turnover.
